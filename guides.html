The Complete Reference Guide to Biologically Appropriate Raw Feeding

Master the biochemistry of Prey Model Raw (PMR 80/10/10) and BARF (70/10/10/5/5) formulation, calcium-to-phosphorus bone ratios, safe 14-day digestive transitions, and kitchen food safety.

1. BARF vs. Prey Model Raw (PMR 80/10/10): Scientific Comparison

When transitioning a dog or cat from extruded commercial kibble to an uncooked, whole-food diet, owners and veterinary nutritionists rely on two foundational formulation standards: Prey Model Raw (PMR) and Biologically Appropriate Raw Food (BARF). While both models eliminate high-heat extrusion, synthetic preservatives, and refined cereal starches, they differ in how they address dietary fiber, phytonutrients, and plant matter.

Nutritional Component Prey Model Raw (PMR 80/10/10) BARF Model (70/10/10/5/5) Puppy Growth Protocol Primary Physiological Role
Muscle Meat & Heart 80% 70% 68% Complete essential amino acids, taurine, L-carnitine, CoQ10, B-vitamins, and bioavailable zinc/iron.
Raw Edible Bone 10% 10% 15% Bioavailable calcium & phosphorus (target 1.2:1 to 1.4:1 Ca:P ratio), marrow lipids, and stool firming.

Other Secreting Organ 5% 5% 6% Kidney, spleen, pancreas, or thymus supplying selenium, Vitamin B12, and enzymatic cofactors.
Low-Glycemic Produce 0% 10% 5% Pureed leafy greens, cruciferous vegetables, and berries for prebiotic fiber and polyphenols.

Why Heart, Gizzards, and Green Tripe Count as Muscle Meat

A common beginner mistake in homemade raw formulation is classifying beef heart, chicken gizzards, or unbleached green tripe as "secreting organs." Anatomically and nutritionally, heart and gizzard are dense striated smooth/cardiac muscle tissues. They do not secrete metabolic hormones or store high concentrations of fat-soluble Vitamin A like liver. However, because cardiac muscle is exceptionally rich in taurine and Coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10), veterinary raw formulators recommend that heart comprise 10% to 15% of the overall daily ration (counted inside the 70%–80% muscle meat bucket).

Pro Formulation Tip: Never exceed 15% heart meat in a single meal, as its dense purine and fat profile can cause loose stools in dogs unaccustomed to rich cardiac tissue.

2. Safe Raw Edible Bone Sizing & Calcium-to-Phosphorus (Ca:P) Balance

Boneless muscle meat is naturally high in phosphorus and severely deficient in calcium (with a Ca:P ratio near 1:18). Left uncorrected, an all-meat diet triggers secondary nutritional hyperparathyroidism, causing the parathyroid gland to leach calcium directly from the dog's skeletal bones. Including 10% actual raw edible bone restores the ancestral 1.2:1 to 1.4:1 calcium-to-phosphorus ratio required by NRC (National Research Council) and AAFCO standards.

CRITICAL SAFETY RULE — NEVER FEED COOKED, SMOKED, OR BOILED BONES: Cooking alters the collagen matrix of bone, turning pliable raw cartilage and calcium into brittle, glass-like shards that can splinter and perforate the esophagus or gastrointestinal tract. Only feed 100% raw, uncooked meaty bones or finely ground raw bone.

Rule of Thumb for RMB Math: Because most poultry Raw Meaty Bones average roughly 33% to 45% pure bone by weight, feeding approximately 25% to 30% of the daily meal as a meaty poultry bone (such as a duck neck or chicken quarter) automatically satisfies the 10% pure bone requirement while contributing the remaining 15%–20% toward the dog's muscle meat allowance.

3. The 14-Day Kibble-to-Raw Digestive Transition Schedule

Dogs fed high-carbohydrate extruded kibble maintain a gastric pH around 2.5 to 3.5 and a gut microbiome adapted to digesting starches. Switching a dog to raw meat, bone, and organs requires lowering gastric pH toward 1.5 to 2.0 (which triggers optimal pepsinogen activation to dissolve raw bone and neutralize foodborne bacteria) and rebuilding proteolytic gut flora.

  1. Days 1–3 (Single Bland Protein + Bone Broth): Start with a single, lean muscle protein (such as skinless turkey breast or lean ground beef heart/chuck) plus a pinch of probiotic or plain goat kefir. Avoid rich secreting organs (liver/kidney) during the first 72 hours.
  2. Days 4–6 (Introduce Soft Edible Bone): Introduce finely ground raw meaty bone (or appropriately sized poultry necks) up to the full 10% pure bone target. Monitor stool consistency—properly balanced bone produces firm, small, low-odor stools.
  3. Days 7–10 (Gradual Liver Introduction — 1% to 5%): Secreting organs are exceptionally nutrient-dense and can cause loose stools if introduced all at once. Start with a sliver of beef or poultry liver (1%–2%) and scale up to the full 5% allowance by Day 10.
  4. Days 11–14 (Add Second Secreting Organ, Omega-3s & Second Protein): Introduce the remaining 5% secreting organ (beef kidney or spleen), Wild Alaskan Salmon Oil (paired with Vitamin E), Organic Kelp Powder, and a second rotational animal protein.
Should You Mix Kibble and Raw in the Same Bowl? If transitioning gradually rather than switching across a 12-hour fast, feed kibble in the morning and the raw portion in the evening (spaced 8–12 hours apart) so the dog's stomach acid can adjust cleanly to each meal type.

4. Closing the 4 Micronutrient Gaps in Commercial Grocery Meat

Wild prey animals consume wild grasses, mineral-rich soil, brains, eyes, thyroid glands, and gut contents. By contrast, supermarket and butcher meats come from grain-fed livestock that have been bled and trimmed of endocrine glands. Even a strict 80/10/10 PMR diet built from grocery store meat will fall short of NRC canine nutritional minimums in four specific micronutrients unless supplemented:

  • 1. Marine Omega-3 Fatty Acids (EPA & DHA): Grain-fed beef and poultry have Omega-6 to Omega-3 ratios as high as 20:1. Supplementing Wild Alaskan Salmon Oil (approx. 1 mL per 10 lbs body weight daily) or whole canned sardines in water restores an anti-inflammatory 4:1 Omega balance.
  • 2. Natural Iodine (Thyroid Support): Because commercial butchers discard the animal's thyroid trachea ring to prevent hyperthyroidism, homemade raw diets require a measured pinch of Organic Kelp Powder (Ascophyllum nodosum) (approx. 1/4 tsp per 25 lbs body weight) for T3/T4 thyroid hormone synthesis.
  • 3. Vitamin E (d-Alpha Tocopherol): Supplementing polyunsaturated marine fish oils increases cellular lipid peroxidation demand. Pairing fish oil with Natural Vitamin E (~2 IU per lb of body weight daily) protects cell membranes and cardiac muscle.
  • 4. Manganese & Joint Glycosaminoglycans: In wild prey, manganese is found in hair, feathers, and green tripe. A deficiency in manganese weakens the cruciate ligaments (CCL/ACL). Adding New Zealand Green-Lipped Mussel Powder (~20 mg per lb body weight) or unbleached green beef tripe supplies bioavailable manganese and chondroitin sulfate.

5. Freezer Prep, Thawing & Kitchen Biosecurity Protocol

Safe raw feeding requires the same strict food-safety hygiene used when preparing raw poultry or steak for human consumption:

  • Deep-Freeze Game & Fish: Freeze wild game, pork, and raw fish at -4°F (-20°C) for at least 7 days prior to feeding to inactivate potential parasites.
  • Refrigerator Thawing Only: Portion bulk monthly batches into airtight 304 stainless steel or glass containers and thaw the next day's rations inside the refrigerator at or below 38°F (3.3°C)—never on a warm countertop.
  • Dedicated Prep Surfaces & Bowl Hygiene: Wash stainless steel dog bowls with hot soapy water after every single meal, and sanitize cutting boards and meat grinders immediately after batch prep.
